# Prototype Baseline Implementation

> **Notice:** This document is a factual inventory of systems currently functioning in the Unity prototype. Feature-specific v1 acceptance is recorded in the [completed task specs](tasks/completed/); other mechanics and art remain provisional and subject to refinement against `docs/concept/`.

## 1. Player & Controls (`unity/Assets/Runtime/Player/`)
- **Controller:** First-person `CharacterController` with smooth crouch (Left Ctrl, 35% speed) and modest sprint (Left Shift, 1.35x speed).
- **Tool ladder:** twelve levels per track (tool, backpack, fuel tank, jetpack) authored once in `EquipmentProgression` and never serialized into `MainGame`; the drill arrives at level 7; a zone's main ground is matched two purchases after the level a player typically owns there; Developer admin → **Tool tuning** nudges bite/cadence/reach live and prints a paste-ready table beside the save directory (`tuning.txt`).
- **Jetpack & Rescue:** hold Space (after a short free-jump window) to thrust; stats come from the Jetpack shop track in `EquipmentProgression.Jetpack` (never the scene): each level climbs faster and cheaper per metre. From level 2 hover hold brakes to a standstill and holds height, draining half the thrust rate, never within half a metre of the floor; by default it holds while dig is held in the air (Developer admin **Hover** switches to "on release", crouch to drop, session-only). Automatic zero-fuel rescue if stranded.
- **Input & Comfort:** Unity Input System with full runtime action rebinding. Hold-to-dig by default with a persisted toggle option. Camera FOV slider (55–90°), crosshair toggle, and preferences persistence (`Preferences/*.ini`).

## 2. Terrain & Excavation (`unity/Assets/Runtime/Terrain/`)
- **Voxel Engine:** Finite signed density field (36 × 26 × 150 m, authored by `SiteLayout`) running Burst-compiled surface-net meshing (0.125 m resolution) with synchronous collision publication. The surface mesh, collider included, rounds every hole's mouth where it meets untouched surface (`TerrainChunkMesh.ShapeMouths`, 0.12 m); density, digging and saves are unchanged. Reusable native workspaces and contiguous density copies bound per-cut work; support-search scratch memory is allocated during loading. Chunks are materialized on demand: 17,550 possible keys, 234 built on a fresh site, so chunk startup and load cost do not scale with depth.
- **Site ground:** the site currently holds one plain ground (soil) everywhere (`SiteLayout.LayeredGround` is off): no zones, places, rooms, pits, odd spots, channels or cracks in play; the Ground Lab keeps every ground. The layered generator below stays for their return.
- **Ground (`TerrainGround`, dormant in the site):** four depth zones (even quarters, undulating borders), each mostly one main ground: soil with gravel lenses; clay; rock veined with winding clay/gravel soft paths; and a zone-4 placeholder of sparser-veined rock. Short mixed bands join the zones, and the top 1.1 m is always soil. Seeded places sit under the plot below 3 m: concrete rubble (zone 1); open-topped concrete structures with soil inside, rock masses and pond-clay basins (zone 2); structures and vein-free rock masses (zone 3). The first structure in zones 2 and 3 (sometimes a second) is a sealed room: roofed concrete with a settled pond-clay silt floor and air above it, carved into the seeded density so it exists from New Game (and after the admin reset) and saves like any cut; its chunks are built with the session, its walls carry denser cracks, and the first tool cut that opens it lets dust drift in (`BrokeIntoRoom`). Seeded pits and columns of backfill (loose mixed refill, most in the recent fill, mostly steep, some leaning) cut across the layers, each with a find waiting at its bottom. Each unique sits in an odd spot: a lens of ground unlike its zone shaped around its reserved envelope (positions copied from the discovery catalog onto `TerrainVolume` by the discovery sync). Winding gravel channels (old riverbeds) run mostly sideways through the soil and clay zones, the first near the plot centre a few metres down. Seeded crack sheets run through rock (densest in rock masses) and concrete: a thin crack line with a fractured band beside it, patches that branch where they cross and thin out at their ends. A Burst job writes the one-byte IDs at session start. Immutable material IDs persist exactly with the hole and are shared by save snapshots without copying; density alone determines occupancy. Surface meshes blend soil grain, compacted clay (orange with grey-blue sediment bands, rust-red below the rock-zone border), packed gravel stones, fractured grey rock (colder in zone 4), pale concrete and smooth grey-blue pond clay from these IDs, with gentle depth colour bands; fractured bands render paler, broken into angular shards, and crack lines as pale mineral seams with dark edges; backfill renders as irregular clay lumps and dark loose stones; world-space mapping and shared halo weights keep chunk edges continuous.
- **Checkpoints:** dense density (~290 MB) plus compact material IDs (~72 MB for the shipped site) are validated and gzip-Fastest encoded on a worker thread, with bounded combined allocation and a 64 MB packed / 512 MB unpacked budget (room for a 200 m site). Only the current format and excavation layout load; previous formats require a new game.
- **Digging:** Hold/toggle repeats shovel scoops through level six, then automatically bores from level seven (a bit pushed along the aim, tip first: a cone 1.2 bite radii long with a rounded tip, widening to the bite and a short collar, its tip a layer past the contact each cut, pushing on up to three layers in one cut while it has taken under 60% of a layer, so it bores in within a few cuts even in hard ground) with sub-cell contact refinement (which steps into ground thinner than two cells, so bridges and overhangs cut too) and matching render/collision updates. Soil yields broad rounded cuts, gravel broad cuts with loose grainy edges, clay narrower smooth shavings, backfill sinking in ~1.5× faster than soil, pond clay wider smooth shavings that bite clearly easier than clay, fractured rock and crack lines crumbling ~1.5× faster than rock, fractured concrete ~2.4× faster than concrete, rock smaller faceted chips, and concrete the smallest square chips. Hardness is mostly bite size and a little cadence (fuel follows cadence); each affected sample keeps its own resistance,; strokes throw no particles. The drill's cross-section takes each ground's cut shape (rock faceted, concrete square, clays elliptical, gravel pebbly, soil and backfill round). A drill cut's axis is the player's aim, not the face it hits, so holes follow the view; each cut stays a thin layer across that axis. Successful tool cuts publish material/position/normal/volume feedback for later audio. Every tool tier can cut every family; upgrades improve output. Detached ground and paper-thin strips clear locally while useful ledges remain. After each tool cut the ground may let go (`ExcavationGrid.TryRelease`, removal only): a cut into a crack's band breaks the connected band loose along the crack (reach grows with the tool), an undercut gravel ceiling pours its section within 3 m, and undercut backfill slumps within 2 m (soil never collapses); each is removed as debris, cleanup follows, finds drop through their own release, and `Released` reports kind, volume and centre. Collection preserves cutting cadence with both tool motions.
- **Admin Tools:** Session-only debug panel (`Ctrl+Shift+F10`) with **Hover: while digging/on release** for the jetpack hover A/B, **Drill size/position** for the first-person drill (its size and its place along the tool), a **Contact shading** slider (the ground contact shading's strength, 0-1.5, for the session; `ContactShading` restores the shared renderer asset when the session ends), tool tuning and **ground tuning** (per-ground bite width/length/depth and stroke time, session-only, printed to `ground-tuning.txt`), selection across all tool levels, refill, and X-ray transparent ground (`Ctrl+Shift+X`). X-ray fades soil and reveals nearby actual finds without changing exposure or collision. **Ground X-ray** (panel only) also fades the ground and marks the tell grounds around the eye with coloured instanced cubes (cracks, gravel, backfill, pond clay; concrete beyond arm's reach), resampled as the player moves or digs; it never changes the grid. Restore normal rules and session load restore opaque ground; the motion always follows the tool level.

## 3. Finds & Physics (`unity/Assets/Runtime/Interaction/`)
- **Finds:** full-size plain rocks form a dense layer immediately beneath the surface, with coal
  entering beneath them; below it one ore ladder spans the whole depth at a roughly constant find
  rate, each type's core band following the zones (copper and iron in the recent fill, silver and
  gold in the sediment, emerald and ruby in the deep stone, diamond at the bottom) with thin scatter
  both ways. Host ground (source-catalog weights per ground family) makes a type denser in its host
  than in other ground at the same depth: ore in rock and rock masses and three times richer again along cracks, metal ores also in gravel, plain rocks in soil.
  Placement uses enclosing spheres around actual visual/collision vertices with shallow soil
  cover; banded placement picks a target depth before searching nearby lateral positions, so
  each band stays populated from its top. On the shipped site, find centres lie beneath the dig
  plot (`SiteLayout.FindFootprint`), keeping the accepted density; the grid beyond holds no finds. Ordinary finds keep out of sealed structures; each room seats 2–5 finds from the matching depth bands a third sunk in its silt, and every backfill pit seats one or two at its bottom; ordinary finds also keep out of each unique's odd spot. Populations are authored in the source catalogs, apply to new games, and saved
  finds keep their positions. Model size and mass remain authored; retired content is deleted.
- **Detection & Pickup:** Ordinary finds use aim-assisted reveal, 60% voxel exposure threshold, held aim pickup between digging ticks and immediately after a revealing cut. `FindProximityCollection` also collects clear finds within a close camera-centred area in front of the player during walking or held digging, at any height, with direct visibility. Released/falling finds have longer aimed reach; station reach stays separate. Aimed and nearby collection can share a held-input frame with its scheduled terrain cut; neither collection nor its animation adds a delay. With a full bag, the cutting ray passes through common finds to reachable ground while walls, equipment and uniques still block it; finds remain physical and recoverable.
- **Release:** Nearly exposed finds with clear interiors and only shallow surface contact become dynamic; substantial burial still anchors them. Gravity and collision determine falling/settling; a slow creep on a slope counts as quiet, so finds stop instead of rolling away forever. Finds cannot be picked up by hand: they are dug, bagged or craned.
- **Prototype uniques:** Independent computer identities reuse Cosmic computer 7, with reserved underground envelopes before common placement. Each exists once per save, requires manually aimed cuts around its covering soil, remains anchored while buried, falls and settles once freed, cannot enter the bag or be sold, and records discovery depth on visible targeting. Uniques are solid to the
  player (commons are not): standing on one carries the player with it (`LoadRide`, so a crane load carries a
  rider up the hole and over to camp), and one moving into the player pushes them aside. Source authoring lives in `art/retro-computer/catalog.json`.
- **Silent detector:** can be switched off for a session (Developer admin → Detector) or for a whole run (`-noDetector`) for the detector-off playtest. A separate bottom-right HUD panel shows three bars based on aim alignment toward a nearby, fully buried eligible find; direct aim gives the strongest level regardless of distance within range. Looking away, leaving range or uncovering any part turns that object's signal off. No arrow, direction/height label or revealed fallback remains. Small angular/range margins prevent flicker; deliberate changes of aim select another source immediately. Commons, observed finds and recovery/storage/display are silent. It costs no fuel or bag space, pauses with gameplay and evaluates only its small authored-source cache each frame, rebuilding that cache when the population changes.
- **Worksite equipment:** New Game gives a few work lamps; further lamps are bought one at a time at the computer (flat price and cap in `EquipmentProgression`) and owned for good (`FpsPlayer.LampKit`), separate from the bag and battery. Rebindable lamp/mark actions preview placement, primary confirms, secondary cancels, and rotate turns markings. The hand-sized lamp goes wherever the player aims (`WorksiteTools.SolveLamp`): upright on floors, spike-first on walls and ceilings, lifted off rough patches, and backed along the aim to the first clear spot when the aimed one is too tight; it anchors only when its base touches fixed scenery, otherwise it falls and settles. Only an empty kit refuses, pointing to the computer. Lamps never collide with the player. Interact retrieves lamps or erases arrow/home/return-here stencils. Terrain edits release unsupported lamps physically and erase paint on removed surfaces; sustained rope-load contact knocks blocking lamps off their mounts. Lamp ownership survives both. Placement never emits light or digs behind the preview.
- **Crane recovery:** Aim at any visible part once sufficiently exposed and hold Interact. A surface glyph previews the eligible point and fills during the hold; completing it bolts a lifting eye on there, which follows the load until it is set down: its base stays flat on the load while its swivel (ball and bow) turns, only in the last ~0.6 s before the hook arrives, so the hook's wire runs through the bow, and swivels toward the hook once hooked, about the base's centre so the hook's seat stays `RecoveryMarkView.HookReach` from the mark. Terrain and objects occlude it; the crane letting go at camp or a planning failure removes it. Marking plans a rope route through connected excavated air to the hole mouth; the salvage crane swings over the mouth (as near as its jib reaches for a mouth beyond it) with its hook at its resting height; as soon as the trolley is aligned over the mouth the hook leaves the hoist where it hangs, swing and all, and rides the smart rope's end in one run straight down under the trolley, down the hole and along the route into the lifting eye (straight from the trolley down the shaft, simulated below it); the crane's cable runs from the trolley's sheaves down the hole and along the route to the hook's block at all times: its two falls hang apart down a straight drop and draw together smoothly where the cable is pulled off the straight line from the trolley to the hook (the rim, a tunnel corner), run along the route as one doubled cable and spread again into the block; each fall keeps the trolley's side, and the block stays square to the trolley's sheaves. The rope pulls the dynamic load at the marked point with a spring guide following the accepted bends. The bounded particle cable retains gravity, inertia and tunnel collision; hauling tension takes up slack without paying the spring's extension back out. Brisk reeling builds stronger force against a jam, ruptures a local patch and releases a fast, freely rotating surge. Hard opposing impacts can immediately break the next dirt contact; the committed removal spends impact energy and only residual momentum continues. Break size follows effort, while soft brushes and nearby untouched soil remain intact. Continued wedging uses short retries and automatically stronger pull; jitter or a creep far slower than the reel cannot reset the jam. The machine's drive builds up for every second the load is stuck (up to a cap) and eases back slowly while it runs free: it pulls harder, reels faster and tears bigger chunks, and a jam that begins with the drive up winds up sooner. Real breaks synchronously update density and mesh/collision and throw pooled debris coloured by the ground there and lit like it: tumbling clods that crumble where they land, crumbs that end where they meet the ground, a short dust puff that slows and thins out, and a brief trickle off the broken face, more for bigger breaks and a harder-driven machine; afterwards dust hangs down in the passage (never over the hole) for a few seconds. Blocking commons loosen without losing identity; mounted lamps can be knocked loose. Permanent obstacles remain intact and player fuel is unaffected. At the top the hook goes back on the hoist and the load hangs from its seat by the lifting eye's link, still a dynamic body that swings and turns (friction in the eye damps it); the hoist eases a load up and down instead of starting and stopping dead; the crane lifts it clear, carries it to the first free set-down spot beside the camp, lowers it slowly and lets go the moment it rests on the ground. Once it has come to rest where it landed it is `Stored`: kinematic, never bagged or sold; aiming at it shows its name, depth and story, Interact repeats it. The load never waits for manual clearance or a retry. Pause/focus loss freezes load and cable and clears transient contact pressure; checkpoints retain the route, the load's pose and motion, the crane's pose and resume recovery automatically.
- **Dense-world cost:** meshes enclosed by pristine soil stop rendering. The test uses each find's placement envelope (its vertex sphere intersected with the renderer bounds), so shallow rocks just under the turf stay hidden; nearby terrain edits reactivate them before small fragments can be missed. Terrain notifications use the existing physics spatial index to refresh nearby finds, with a complete scan on query overflow for large edits/reset/restore. Exposure uses a Burst-compiled batch over each find's local density neighborhood and one shared terrain workspace; support checks share the transform and fast density interpolation. Route planning has both work and elapsed-time limits, with at most one slice per rendered frame before the crane starts. Anchored physics callbacks sleep until a terrain change or explicit restore; collision and save records remain intact.

## 4. Hub & Economy (`unity/Assets/Runtime/Player/`, `Runtime/Interaction/`)
- **Surface computer:** one Cosmic retro terminal opens selling for a carried haul, then upgrades immediately after Sell All or the last individual sale; an empty bag opens upgrades directly. The prompt reads simply `Use`, with no key prefix. Tool, battery and bag each have ten levels with independent sequential purchases from `EquipmentProgression` and its shared price ladder; parameters stay in code. The tool row previews the shovel-to-drill milestone; final-level rows remain visible and cannot charge again. The Services column sells fuel refills and single work lamps.
- **Refill Economy:** Paid battery recharge ($1 minimum, whole-dollar `$`).
- **Salvage crane:** the purchased tower crane (the pack's smallest model, weathered) stands just outside the plot's south-east edge beside the camp; its trolley reaches about three quarters of the plot and three set-down spots east of the camp; for a hole mouth beyond its reach it parks as near as it reaches and the rope runs across to the hole. Its jib rests over the set-down spots. It is driven through the pack's own levers (slew, trolley, hoist, hook turn) by `TowerCraneRig` with the pack's speed knob raised and an anti-sway assist that settles the hook under the trolley; the pack's straight hoist cable is stripped from its mesh and its two falls are redrawn as the pack's strands, so they can bend; its slewing colliders ignore raycasts; the vendor controller scripts are stripped from the project prefab variant. Recovered uniques stay where it set them down.

## 5. UI & Presentation (`unity/Assets/Runtime/UI/`)
- **UI Toolkit:** Single UI Document (`FpsHud`) driving the HUD, Pause menu, Settings tabs, and Station trading interfaces with unified grayscale styling.
- **Capacity warning:** Full inventory uses the same persistent resource banner as low fuel; simultaneous warnings stack above the bottom edge, without overlapping feedback.
- **Focus loss:** the game still pauses when the window loses focus, but the dim overlay and pause card are hidden while focus is elsewhere, so external screenshot tools capture the game rather than the pause screen.
- **Frame pacing:** the default frame limit follows the monitor's refresh rate ("Display"), including before the scene loads; saved limits or VSync take over once preferences load. An unfocused player window renders at most 30 FPS.
- **Resolution:** rendering defaults to 100%; the Graphics render-resolution row (50–100%) upscales with FSR. Borderless startup and switching use the current monitor's desktop resolution and aspect ratio; its resolution row reads Desktop. Fullscreen/windowed modes retain supported output choices, including 4K and higher, with timed Keep/Revert confirmation.
- **Graphics settings:** quality preset (Low/Medium/High/Ultra, Custom), Test this PC, render resolution, view distance, sun shadows (Off/Low/Medium/High), anti-aliasing (Off/FXAA/2×/4×/8× MSAA), ambient occlusion, texture mip quality and anisotropic filtering apply immediately and persist as device preferences. High is the default and the accepted look. A player's first launch measures the PC at the title (`GraphicsAutoTuner`) and stores a recommended preset and resolution; the Graphics reset returns to it. View distance uses global terrain quality overrides (High keeps the authored grass distances and shades all visible terrain in full, without the blurry distant basemap) and the global Mesh LOD threshold: finds carry generated index-only detail levels, so lower tiers switch them to simpler meshes sooner; ambient occlusion Off selects a second renderer without contact shading. Sun shadows use a smaller two-cascade map with smooth filtering, with coarser Medium/Low tiers on the runtime URP clone; local lamp occlusion and the excavation daylight field remain active at every quality level. Depth priming is disabled, so MSAA Off renders the same surfaces as the multisampled settings. Ground contact shading (SSAO) runs after opaques at half resolution, so no depth prepass renders; scene depth for water is copied after opaques. The dig-ground shader samples only the triplanar projections that carry weight. Graphics rows act only through global pipeline, camera and quality state, so new content follows them (rules in `unity/readme.md`).
- **Computer screens:** selling and upgrades share the existing fixed-size parts-board table (`Station.uss` + `ToolkitStationRows`) — money-only header, categories in their own columns, one clickable row per upgrade track, refill service or carried find. One click buys; nothing is selected first and nothing resizes.
- **Drained lakebed site:** generated by `LakebedSiteSetup` from the Highlands demo canyon: terrain,
  vendor scenery instances, lake, streams, islands, plants and debris. Rerun it instead of hand-editing
  (see `unity/readme.md`); design decisions live in the [089 spec](tasks/completed/089-highlands-drained-lakebed.md).
  The plot outline comes from `SiteLayout`; a terrain hole under the permanent rim collar exposes it,
  and the collar roofs the rest of the grid for lateral digging. Stream water (`Trickles`) draws just
  before the lake. Walls and a flight ceiling on the Ignore Raycast layer keep the player on the
  drained section, and scenery unseen from there is removed at setup. Scenery reports the
  permanent-boundary prompt; the terrain casts no shadows (casters ignore holes). Detail switches
  happen only far from the player; terrain grass (at High view distance) and anything in or near the play area are never
  culled from inside it (`LakebedSiteSetup.PlayViewDistance`). Baked occlusion uses permanent rocks only, never excavation,
  terrain or foliage. The stations sit on the camp arc south of the plot.
- **Dig ground and soil:** the plot's untouched top is the Highlands mud, evenly and slightly
  darkened (the cap); up close it carries the dug soil's grain (`CapGrain`). It covers only near-flat faces and
  fades into the soil across a hole's rounded mouth. Beyond the outline the collar and the terrain (`DigCap` layer) lighten it into the damp band
  (`DampMud`) within a couple of metres with one shared share map (`DigBandShare`), and the band fades
  into the lighter lakebed within about ten. Patches of the green turf with the canyon's green grass
  blades ring the plot from a few metres beyond the tape (`LakebedSiteSetup.GreenPatch`), sparser on
  the camp's side. The
  collar's inner edge bevels up from the dig surface, so no step shows along the outline, and the
  physical edge wobbles around the smooth find footprint (`SiteLayout.OpeningRadius` vs
  `FootprintRadius`). Freshly cut topsoil is clay loam (the Mountains mud, tinted); clay, gravel, rock
  and concrete textures lie below, blended from saved material IDs. Terrain grass is one turf (`DryTurf`),
  tinted to the canyon plants' warm meadow olive (`MeadowGrass` and kin), as are the rocks' painted grass tops
  (`Content/Lakebed/Rocks` copies); terrain ash trees (the ones with painted roots) whose foot shows wherever their trunk is seen from the ground
  in the play area stand 1.1 m deeper (times their height scale), and knee-high grass grows round every tree's
  feet, hiding the pack's lime-painted root flare; trees half behind rocks keep their height and the bushes (`BushLeaves`, which sway with the grass); canyon plants stay under 1.2x scale; the demo's two grass layers are dropped. Beyond the edge
  the top 1.2 m is a permanent soil bank (`SiteLayout.BankDepth`, enforced on tool cuts by
  `ExcavationGrid`), so pit edges are soil walls; find centres stay 0.5 m inside the edge. The canyon otherwise keeps the demo's layers
  and paint.
- **Dig boundary:** one creased red-and-white barrier tape tied round 10 cm timber posts, each cast into a square
  concrete footing, on the collar outlines the plot (one static, collider-free mesh, open toward the camp).
- **Presentation:** the Highlands sky, grade and haze with a noon sun (`SunPresentationSetup`); hue curves calm the packs' lime greens and lean them toward yellow.
  Lighting and water refresh without regenerating the site, keeping Inspector tuning. `WindowsBuild`
  always targets MainGame.
- **Tool rig:** the tool rises from the bottom edge right of centre, no hands: the purchased Stylized Western
  Shovel (`art/stylized-western-shovel`) at every shovel level and the purchased Hand Mining Drill
  (`art/hand-mining-drill`, its screw head on a spin pivot seated on the body, the model's shaft cut from its mesh) at every drill level, both placed by Configure Tool Rig with
  project URP materials. Parts are named by the tool levels they appear at; a purchase or admin level swaps them
  instantly. Each shovel stroke pries the soil loose and scoops it: the stroke starts on the press and the dirt is
  removed at the scoop, when the blade lifts. Its visible head sits within about 0.3 m of the eye, inside the player's
  capsule (never clips walls), keeps its screen place at any FOV (the drill sits further forward and tipped down, so its
  body shows and its head stays off the crosshair), casts no shadow and is lit only like the world. Only the tool moves: a pry-and-scoop stroke
  per dig up to level 6 (harder ground pushes it less), a spinning bit and chatter from the drill on; it lowers
  away for menus, placement previews and recovery marking. Re-run **Configure Tool Rig**
  after changing either model or its project textures.
- **Underground lighting:** `ExcavationDaylight` derives daylight from the connected excavated route, tallying descent (straight or sloped) and sideways/climbing travel separately: descent fades a shaft or walked ramp from the first metres (dim by about 10 m, dark by about 20 m), while every metre sideways dims it at once, so side tunnels darken within a few metres at any depth. Its nodes are 0.5 m apart; a node whose own point is buried stands at the most open point within 0.2 m of it, so a hole narrower than the spacing still carries light down it (links between nodes still need clear air). A cut lights its fresh ground at once from its lit neighbours; the timesliced route rebuild then covers only the excavated part of the grid and replaces those values. Dug ground, adapted URP Lit finds/boundaries and soil debris get only the scene's sun and daylight fill, both following the route's daylight (with reflections); the fill is a gradient ambient (`SunPresentationSetup.ConfigureAmbient`: sky above, sunlit-ground bounce at the horizon and below), so pit walls are lit like real walls under the high sun. There is no extra fill inside holes, so a hole is lit like the ground around it and a deep or narrow one darkens naturally. The surface is untouched, sealed rooms admit no daylight and there is no ambient brightness floor.
- **Work lights:** small caged lanterns light a wide area in every direction with local soft shadows; each light's close-range brightness is capped in the ground/find shaders, so output buys reach, not a hot spot. A lamp shines from a point a little out along its axis (never inside nearby ground), so floor lamps light the floor. Lamp shadows come from the ground only (the lamp's shadow rendering layer, `TerrainVolume.LampShadowLayer`, set on every ground chunk; rendering layers are enabled in the URP asset), so finds resting near a lamp never throw room-sized shadows; the sun still takes shadows from everything. The light point keeps clear space from anything touching the lamp and is re-aimed every frame; a loose lamp throws it straight up. Only the nearest eight lamps within the cull distance shine, fading in and out; the rest keep their glowing dome. Range plus cull distance equals the sun shadow distance. No personal light, fuel drain or expiry. Marks conform to collision surfaces and remain readable by their shape when lit.

## 6. Persistence & Lifecycle (`unity/Assets/Runtime/Persistence/`)
- **Saving:** Current-format v16 whole-world snapshots (`WorldSaveController`), atomic disk write, 10 s background autosave, recovery from interruptions. Unchanged find records are reused between checkpoints, keeping the main-thread capture small. Find lifecycle, the recovery (accepted rope route, attachment, payload pose/velocities, guide progress, the crane's pose, grab point and set-down spot), owned lamp count, lamp poses/motion and route marks share the terrain checkpoint; invalid ownership or progress is rejected.
- **Windows Process:** Single-instance reservation (`DesktopInstance`) focusing existing window on relaunch.
- **Ground Lab (development builds):** the title menu's **Ground Lab** refills the site grid with labelled 3 x 3 m bays (every ground alone plus mixes that show a pour, slumps, crack breaks and zone changes; `GroundLab`) and six dug crane scenes around them in soil (`GroundLab.Crane`: straight, narrow and rock-lined shafts, an open pit, a deep shaft, a bent tunnel), each with a computer in a pocket ready to mark (the catalog computers repeat; six wall rocks in one shaft). Lab air is carved on every reset and its chunks are built up front. It starts with unlimited battery and never saves (`WorldSaveState.Lab`); the crosshair prompt names the crane scene or bay and the ground hit; set-down computers clear from camp after 5 s; **Restart Ground Lab** reloads MainGame straight back into the lab and **Leave Ground Lab** reloads MainGame.
